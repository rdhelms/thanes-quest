# Thane's Quest

`README.md` describes the game as it is today.

## The vision

The quality, scale, depth, story, feel, and fun of the game should be awe-inspiring. Someone who
plays it should say "wow". It should be popular, good enough that people would want to pay for it
whether or not it is ever sold, and a game its owner is proud of and enjoys playing.

**It is for anyone.** That includes people who remember King's Quest and Quest for Glory, people
who do not play many games and came across this one, and people who love games but never played
the old ones. It is family friendly, for ages thirteen and up.

**Typing is optional, and it is the richer way to play.** It lets a player explore the world
fully and try creative actions that a point-and-click or console game could not allow. It is less
convenient than the other controls, so a player who wants to use only lower-friction controls can
play the game that way. A player who chooses to type gets more for it: Easter eggs, hidden
puzzles, jokes, and more interesting stories.

**The game should feel both deep and vast.** The Sierra adventure games, Skyrim, and D&D are
examples of games that achieve that feeling of depth and scale, each in a different way.

**Its stories should affect players deeply** and stay with them after they stop playing.

## How to write its stories

What professional writers and game designers say makes a story work. These hold for every story in
the game, the main one and the side ones.

**Character**

- Someone must want something, and it must cost them. Vonnegut: every character should want
  something, even if only a glass of water.
- True character is revealed in the choices a person makes under pressure (Robert McKee).
- The conflict worth writing is internal: "the human heart in conflict with itself" (Faulkner).

**Plot**

- Cause, not sequence. "The king died and then the queen died" is a story; "the king died, and
  then the queen died of grief" is a plot (E. M. Forster). Between any two beats it should be
  possible to say "therefore" or "but", never "and then" (Trey Parker and Matt Stone).
- An ending should be surprising yet inevitable (Aristotle). Coincidence may get characters into
  trouble, never out of it (Pixar).

**World**

- Magic is defined by its limits and costs (Brandon Sanderson). Problems can only be solved with
  magic the audience already understands, limitations are more interesting than powers, and what
  exists should be deepened before anything new is added.
- Know far more than is shown (Hemingway's iceberg). Tolkien's sense of depth comes from glimpses
  of histories that are never told.

**Telling**

- Give the audience two plus two, not four: let them do the sum (Andrew Stanton). Know what the
  story is about in one sentence, and never have anyone say it.
- People talk to get something, and rarely say what they mean. Each voice should be recognisable
  without the name attached.

**Games**

- The player is the protagonist. The story is what they do and find, not what they are told. A
  place should make the player ask "what happened here?" and let them work it out (Harvey Smith
  and Matthias Worch, on environmental storytelling).
- In-world sources are partial and biased. The Elder Scrolls tells its history through books that
  disagree with each other. A legend is somebody's version.
- Obstacles come out of the story and move it forward. No arbitrary puzzles, and reward the
  player's intent (Ron Gilbert, "Why Adventure Games Suck").

## How to work here

There is no backlog, on purpose. Play the game before changing it, and decide for yourself what
would do most to bring it closer to the vision. You are free to change how the game is built today
and to disagree with what an earlier session decided.

Nobody is playing the game yet, so merge your own work into `main` when you are satisfied with it.
A push to `main` deploys the live game. This is to be revisited once real people are playing.

Do not copy another game's protected material (its characters, art, music, text, or trademarks).

Keep the README accurate and short: only what someone needs to run, play, and build on the game.

`STORY.md` tells the game's stories, the main one and the side ones. It holds only what is in the
game: no plans and no ideas for later. Keep it and the game in step, and leave neither with an
unfinished storyline or plot holes.
